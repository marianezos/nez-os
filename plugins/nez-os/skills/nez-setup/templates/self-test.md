# Self-test scenarios

Pick the scenario that fits the agent's main client type, so the test checks what they actually do. Use My specialty if it names a client type, otherwise the first item in Client types I work with, otherwise Mostly buyers, sellers or both. If you can't tell, use the buyer scenario and say so.

| Main client type | Scenario |
| --- | --- |
| Sellers, luxury | Seller wants a higher price |
| Buyers, first-time buyers, new construction, relocation, 55+ communities | Buyer waiting on rates |
| Investors | Investor says the numbers don't work |
| Landlords | Landlord wants higher rent |
| Renters | Renter on a tight budget |
| Commercial, land | Business owner says the cost is too high |

## Filling in the details

- **[AREA]:** the first Market Areas row, otherwise the main market.
- **[PRICE]:** a realistic example inside their typical price range. If it's blank, ask once: "What's a typical price in [main market]?"
- **[HIGHER PRICE]:** about 8 to 10 percent above [PRICE].
- **[RENT] / [LOWER RENT]:** realistic monthly rents for their market, about 15 percent apart. Ask once if you have no basis.
- Tell the agent which scenario and which example numbers you picked, and that the numbers are made up for the test.

## Deliverables for every scenario

1 and 2 are listed under each scenario. 3 and 4 are always:

3. **A follow-up email they could send tonight:** under 150 words, in their voice, ending with the full disclosure line and any required notices for email.
4. **One social caption on the topic:** Fair Housing safe, no promises, ending with the short disclosure line.

If the Objection Bank has a row for the objection in the scenario, use it and say so in the grade.

## Seller wants a higher price
"I just left a listing appointment in [AREA]. The comps point to about [PRICE], but the sellers want [HIGHER PRICE]. They've lived there a long time and love the home. Two other agents are also interviewing."
1. **Pricing plan:** how to talk about the gap without arguing (3 bullets).
2. **The attachment:** two things I could say that respect how they feel about the home.

## Buyer waiting on rates
"I met buyers who love a home in [AREA] listed around [PRICE]. They want to wait for rates to drop before offering, and they're not sure about signing a buyer agreement. Two other agents are in the picture."
1. **Talking about the wait:** how to discuss timing without predicting rates (3 bullets).
2. **The buyer agreement:** two things I could say about their hesitation.

## Investor says the numbers don't work
"An investor is looking at a property in [AREA] listed around [PRICE]. They say the numbers don't work at that price and that they can find deals on their own. Two other agents want their business."
1. **Working the numbers:** how to walk through the deal without promising returns (3 bullets).
2. **"I can find deals myself":** two things I could say.

## Landlord wants higher rent
"A landlord in [AREA] wants about [RENT] a month, but similar places rent for about [LOWER RENT]. They'd also rather manage it themselves. Two other agents have pitched them."
1. **Pricing the rent:** how to show the gap using the market, without guarantees (3 bullets).
2. **"I'll manage it myself":** two things I could say.

## Renter on a tight budget
"A renter looking in [AREA] has a tight budget and is worried about the deposit, fees and their credit. They say they might just search on their own."
1. **The full cost:** how to lay out move-in costs and set honest expectations (3 bullets).
2. **"I'll look on my own":** two things I could say.

## Business owner says the cost is too high
"A business owner in [AREA] is looking at a space quoted around [PRICE or RENT]. They say it costs too much, they'd rather deal with the owner directly, and they're unsure how long a commitment to make."
1. **Total cost and terms:** how to compare options the same way and negotiate, without guessing at legal terms (3 bullets).
2. **"I'll deal directly":** two things I could say.
