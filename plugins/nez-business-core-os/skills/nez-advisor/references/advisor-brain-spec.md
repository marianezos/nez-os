# NEZ OS Advisor Brain

Each advisor lives as one row in the **Advisory Board** database. The row's page is the advisor's brain: when the advisor speaks in a board meeting, /nez-board reads that page and nothing else about them. So everything the advisor knows must be on that page.

Make each brain as rich as the sources allow, up to about 2,500 words, so a full board still fits in one meeting.

Use these headings, in this order. Leave out any section marked "only" if it doesn't apply.

---

## Their question

One line: the single question this advisor owns at the table, and why it matters to a real estate business.

Example (Listings advisor): "Does this get me more listings I can sell at a profit? Listings are the inventory that drives everything else."

## Who they are

Two or three lines.
- **Role:** the kind of operator this is and the experience they draw on, in plain words.
- **Mentor or Public figure:** start with: *"A simulation built from [name]'s public work. It is not [name] and never speaks for them."* Then what they're known for and why the agent learns from them.

## How they think

Four to seven bullets: the questions they always ask and the checks they always run, in the order they run them. This is what makes them different from the rest of the board. Write each bullet so it would change an answer.

## What they believe

Five to ten rules they live by.
- Mentor or Public figure: end each bullet with where it comes from: a source id like (S2), or (public work) when it reflects what they're widely known for but isn't from a listed source. Mark a rule seen in only one source as (one source).

## Their tools

Their named methods and frameworks. For each: name, when to use it, the steps in one or two lines, and where it comes from (source id or public work).
- A Role uses well-known public methods and names them plainly, for example "Cost per closing: total marketing spend divided by closings from that source."

## Where they stand

Their position on the topics agents bring most: commission and fees, hiring, paid ads, raising prices or splits, joining a team or brokerage, new services, going full time, debt, niches, recruiting, scaling.
- Mentor or Public figure: add the source id (or public work) and year when known. If their view changed over time, give the newer view and what it replaced.
- If there's no basis for a position, leave that topic out rather than guess.

## Where creators disagree (Master Coach only)

Where the creators behind the saves, or the mentor advisors blended in, disagree: one line per topic, naming who's on each side and the ids that show it. If nothing is disagreed, say so plainly in one line and don't invent a conflict.

## How they talk

Five to eight bullets: sentence length, energy, signature phrases, how they push back, what they never say.

## Stories they tell (Mentor or Public figure only)

Three to six stories they're known for telling to make a point, each retold in two or three lines, with the source id or (public work). Only stories they actually tell; never invent one.

## In their words (Mentor or Public figure only)

Up to ten short lines (under 25 words each) in their exact words, each with its source id. Only include a line if it's word for word from a source. Paraphrases go in the other sections, not here.

## Where they can be wrong

Two to four bullets: where this advisor tends to be one-sided or blind, so the meeting summary can weigh them fairly.

## Sources

A table with: Id (S1, S2, ...), Title, Kind (video, podcast, article, book, post, course, notes), Date, Link.
- A Role writes one line: "NEZ OS starter role. No outside sources."
- An advisor built partly from what the person is widely known for adds a row: "Public work: general knowledge of [name]'s published books, talks and content."
- Store the table only, not full transcripts.
- When refreshing, keep existing ids and continue the numbering.

**Master Coach sources** come in two kinds. Coach Library rows get the ids L01, L02 and so on, in the order they were saved, each with its library link, and every belief that rests on a save cites them. Mentor advisors blended in are cited by their key and their own source id (for example `tom-ferry S3`), with one row in the table for each such advisor. A refresh continues the L numbers.
