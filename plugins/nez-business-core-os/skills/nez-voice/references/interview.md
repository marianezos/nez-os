# Samples and interview

Used by SKILL.md Steps 3 and 4. Look first: skip anything the Master Profile, the Voice Library page, My personality and the samples already answer, and show what you noticed as statements the agent can correct.

## What counts as a sample

Only things the person wrote or said themselves:
- Emails and texts they sent, social posts and captions, blog posts, newsletters, listing descriptions they wrote.
- Video, YouTube, podcast or voice memo transcripts.
- Aim for at least 2 kinds of writing plus 1 spoken transcript if they have one. Different audiences (clients, friends, team, social) show how the voice shifts.
- Pasted text and uploaded files are both fine. 5 to 10 pieces across kinds is enough to start. More helps, but never push for more than they want to give.

## What doesn't count

- Anything written by an AI, a ghostwriter, an assistant or a team member.
- Copied text, templates, forwarded messages, and brokerage or MLS boilerplate.
- Marketing copy someone else wrote for them.

If a sample looks AI-written (openers like "I hope this email finds you well," a perfect structure, a list of three wrapped up neatly, em dashes they don't use elsewhere, or it just reads very differently from the rest), say so in one line and ask: "This one reads differently from your others. Did you write it yourself? Should I use it?" Use it only if they say yes.

## Sent mail (optional, read-only)

Only if an email tool is connected and the agent agrees. Search sent mail for a mix of people (a client, a friend, a teammate, a lender). Show the list (subject, date, and who it went to in general terms, like "a buyer client") and let them remove any. Read only the ones they approve. Never send, draft, label or change anything. Say what you used when you're done.

## Interview: up to four questions at a time

Ask only what the samples and the look-first step didn't answer.

1. **Phrases you use a lot.** What do you say all the time? How do you greet people, and how do you sign off?
2. **Words you'd never use.** What words make you cringe? What would you never say? (Real estate clichés like "dream home" or "won't last long" count.)
3. **Humor.** Do you joke? What kind: dry, silly, poking fun at yourself, none? Where do you keep it out?
4. **Emoji and punctuation.** Which emoji, how many, where? Exclamation points, "...", capital letters, all lowercase, dashes?
5. **Formality by audience.** How do you sound with clients, friends, on social, and with your team or lender?
6. **Openings and closings.** How do you start and end an email, a text, a post, a video?
7. **Stories.** Do you tell stories? Short or long? Do you lead with the point or build up to it?
8. **Slang and local words.** Any slang, local phrases or neighborhood nicknames you use?
9. **Languages.** Which languages do you write in? Do you mix them, and when?
10. **Profanity.** Do you swear in your own writing or speech? Yes or no, and where?
11. **How you want to come across.** Warm, direct, funny, calm, bold? What should a reader feel?
12. **Anything else** a ghostwriter should know about how you write.

## Show what you noticed

After each batch, list what you noticed in short statements and ask if they're right, for example: "Your texts are short, no capital at the start, and you sign with just your first name. Right?" Keep what they confirm; drop what they correct.
