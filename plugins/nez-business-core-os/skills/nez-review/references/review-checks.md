# Review checks

Used by `SKILL.md`. These rules keep every review honest and the same from one run to the next.

## 1. What data a review can use

- **The plan:** a quarter plan from /nez-goals if one exists. Otherwise the weekly and monthly numbers in the math table on Business Plan & Goals, and This quarter's target closings, labeled (your plan, spread evenly by week).
- **Actuals:** the lines under Weekly check-ins on Business Plan & Goals, Closed rows in Clients & Deals (Key Date in the timeframe, confirmed with the agent), and anything the agent gives during the review. Never rewrite a past check-in.
- **In progress:** Under contract rows in Clients & Deals, shown as "not counted yet."
- **By client type:** check-in lines that name a type, and the Type on Closed deals. Everything else is a gap.
- **By lead source:** check-in lines with a source tally (written as `Sources: Sphere 4, Open house 2`), the Lead Source on Clients & Deals rows, or rough counts the agent gives now, labeled (your estimate).
- **Take-home:** only the pay or cost lines saved in My business numbers.
- **Earlier reviews:** the Business Reviews page.

## 2. Enough data?

- **Fewer than two check-ins** in the timeframe: the review is early. Say so, work from what exists, and don't call trends.
- **No source data anywhere:** ask once for rough counts, offer to skip, and if skipped write "Not covered: no lead source data yet."
- **Too few to tell:** don't call a source or client type strong or weak with fewer than 5 leads or 3 closings. Say "too few to tell" and show the counts.
- **Missing numbers** are never filled in. Say what's missing and what it would unlock ("With your monthly ad spend I can show cost per closing").

## 3. Status words

Compare actual with plan to date for each number.

| Status | When |
| --- | --- |
| Ahead | Actual is more than 10% above plan |
| On track | Actual is within 10% of plan |
| Behind | Actual is more than 10% below plan |
| Not counted yet | The work is still in progress because of the time from first contact to closing (under contract, or activity too recent to close) |

Say once that the 10% line is NEZ OS's rule, not theirs, and offer to use a different one. If they pick another, use it for the whole review and note it on the review.

## 4. Lead source view

For each source:
- Leads, agreements, closings.
- **Lead to close rate** = closings ÷ leads × 100.
- Compare with their overall lead to close rate from My business numbers, labeled (from your own numbers).
- Compare its share of closings with its place in Where my business comes from. A source doing better than its rank, or worse, is worth saying out loud.
- **Cost per closing** = monthly spend × months in the timeframe ÷ closings. Show the arithmetic. Spend but no closings: "cost per closing can't be worked out yet," with the spend shown.
- A source with no cost entered shows "no cost entered" (normal for sphere and referrals).
- Never compare with a per-source target. There aren't any.

## 5. Finding the bottleneck

Use their own rates from My business numbers. Name one.

1. **Not enough activity:** activity is more than 10% below plan, and their rates are in line with their own. The fix is more of that activity.
2. **Activity that isn't converting:** activity is on plan or ahead, but the rate to the next step is more than 10% below their own rate. The fix is that step (name it: lead to appointment, appointment to agreement, or agreement to close).
3. **Timing:** results are low only because of the time from first contact to closing, while activity and rates are fine. The fix is patience and keeping the activity going. Say what should close and when.

If two apply, name the one earliest in the funnel.

## 6. Keep, fix or drop

- **Keep:** at or ahead of plan, at or better than their own rate, or the best return.
- **Fix:** producing, but below their rate, or costing more per closing than their other sources.
- **Drop:** has enough data to judge, produced nothing (or the worst return) for the whole timeframe, and dropping it frees time or money for something in Keep. Always say where the freed time or money goes. Never drop on a sample below the "too few to tell" rule.

## 7. Wording

- Plain words. The agent should be able to check every line.
- A number next to every claim.
- No promises about future results. Say what the numbers show and what to watch.
- No em dashes.
