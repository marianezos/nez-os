# Ranking

Used by `SKILL.md` Step 4.

## The score

For each bottleneck:

- **Hours per week** = time each time × how often, turned into a week. Show it ("45 minutes × 4 a week = 3 hours"). If more than one person does it, add their time and say so.
- **Pain (1 to 5):** how much it hurts. Ask the agent. 1 is a small annoyance; 5 causes mistakes, lost clients or stress.
- **Automatable (1 to 5):** how much of it a tool can do. You judge, give the reason in one line, and the agent can change it. 1 is all judgment or relationship; 5 is the same steps every time. Never raise it because a tool is exciting.
- **Score** = hours per week × pain × automatable. Show the three numbers and the result.

## Show the top 3 to 5

A short table (Bottleneck, Owner, Hours per Week, Pain, Automatable, Score) and one sentence of reason each ("most hours, and it's the same steps every time"). Ties: the one with the lighter fix first. Never more than five. Save the rest as Open in Bottlenecks for later.

## Keep human

Tasks they love, and tasks that need judgment, a client relationship or a compliance call, are listed separately as Keep human with the reason, whatever their score. A high score on one of these is a prompt to ask whether a smaller part can be handed off (the prep before a listing appointment, not the appointment itself).

## Estimated hours saved

For each recommended fix, a range:

- **Low** = the hours per week of the steps the fix fully handles, minus the time a person still spends reviewing or approving.
- **High** = the low number plus the hours of the steps the fix partly handles.
- Use their own numbers for each step. A step with no number: ask, or leave it out and say so.
- Label it: "Estimate, not a promise." Never a standard percentage, a benchmark or a number from memory.
- Ask them to note how long the task takes after the fix for two weeks. Record it as Actual Hours Saved on the next run.

## What an hour is worth (money saved)

Show hours saved in dollars too, so the choice is easier.

- **Value of my hour:** use the line in My business numbers if it's saved. If not, work it out from what the brain already has: Last year: GCI (or revenue) ÷ (weekly working hours × 50 weeks). Weekly working hours come from Hours I work and hours I protect (My boundaries and How I work). Show the math. Only if a number is missing, ask once: "About what is an hour of your time worth to your business? A rough number is fine, or skip it." Save the result to **Value of my hour** in My business numbers. Skipped: show hours only.
- **Money saved per month** = the hours saved range × Value of my hour × 4.3. Show it next to the hours, for example "4 to 6 hours a week, about $1,400 to $2,100 a month." Label it "Estimate, not a promise."

## What the fix costs

For each recommended fix, ask once: "Does this fix cost anything, like a tool, an add-on or paying someone? About how much a month, and how many hours to set up?" Skippable.

- Save the answer to **Fix Cost** on the Bottlenecks row (for example "$97 a month, 2 hours to set up").
- **Net value per month** = money saved per month minus the monthly cost (show it). If it's close to zero or negative, say so plainly and suggest a lighter rung.
- Rungs a and b usually cost nothing. Say "no cost" and don't ask.
- **Not sure yet:** that's fine. Write "Not known yet" in Fix Cost, show the fix with "cost not known yet," and add an Open Questions row ("Cost of [fix] for [bottleneck]", Area Growth, Where It Goes "Bottlenecks: [bottleneck] (Fix Cost)", Found By /nez-bottleneck). /nez-next brings it back later, and when they answer, update Fix Cost and the Open Question. Never guess a price.
