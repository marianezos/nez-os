# Starter objections: First-time buyers

NEZ OS starters for Stage 5. Rewrite each answer in the agent's voice, swap [PROOF] for one of their proof points (or [ADD PROOF]), and add the pushback they hear most. Programs, approval and credit questions go to a lender. Never promise approval or a program.

## 1. "We don't have enough saved for a down payment."
- **Category:** Money & readiness
- **Behind it:** They think they need 20 percent, or they don't know the options.
- **Ask first:** "What amount do you think you need to get started?"
- **Answer idea:** Many first-time buyers need less than they think, and some areas have down payment help. A lender can tell them what they may qualify for. [PROOF] Offer to connect them with a lender, or to a class or guide if you have one.
- **Text:** You may need less than you think. Want me to connect you with a lender who can show you the options? No commitment.
- **Don't say:** "You'll definitely qualify." Specific program amounts unless they came from the lender.

## 2. "My credit isn't good enough."
- **Category:** Money & readiness
- **Behind it:** Embarrassment, or an old belief about what lenders need.
- **Ask first:** "Has a lender looked at it yet, or is that your best guess?"
- **Answer idea:** Lots of buyers start here. A lender can tell them where they stand and what to work on, and many people are closer than they think. [PROOF] Offer a no-pressure lender intro and to stay in touch while they prepare.
- **Text:** Lots of people start right where you are. Want a lender to take a quick look so you know exactly where you stand?
- **Don't say:** Credit advice or score targets. "That won't be a problem."

## 3. "Renting is cheaper right now."
- **Category:** Money & readiness
- **Behind it:** They're comparing rent to a full mortgage payment and feel unsure.
- **Ask first:** "What would owning need to give you for it to be worth it?"
- **Answer idea:** Sometimes renting is the right choice for now, and that's fine. Offer to compare their real rent to a real payment from a lender, including what they'd be building over time. [PROOF] Let them decide with their own numbers.
- **Text:** Renting might be the right call for now. Want to compare your rent to a real payment so you can decide with numbers?
- **Don't say:** "Renting is throwing money away." Any promise about appreciation or equity.

## 4. "We're scared of making a mistake."
- **Category:** Trust & process
- **Behind it:** Buying feels huge and they don't know the steps.
- **Ask first:** "What part worries you most?"
- **Answer idea:** Name the fear as normal. Walk them through the steps in order so nothing is a surprise, and explain where they can pause or walk away (inspection, appraisal, financing). [PROOF] Offer your step-by-step guide or a short call.
- **Text:** Totally normal to feel that way. Want me to send the steps from start to keys so you know what's coming?
- **Don't say:** "Don't worry about it." Anything that brushes off the fear.

## 5. "We don't know if we're ready to buy."
- **Category:** Money & readiness
- **Behind it:** They don't know what 'ready' looks like.
- **Ask first:** "What would 'ready' look like to you?"
- **Answer idea:** Offer a simple readiness check with a lender and you: savings, credit, payment comfort, timeline. Then a plan, whether that's now or in a year. [PROOF]
- **Text:** No rush. Want a quick readiness check so you know exactly where you stand?
- **Don't say:** Pressure. 'Now is the best time to buy.'

## 6. "The process feels overwhelming."
- **Category:** Trust & process
- **Behind it:** Too many steps and unfamiliar words.
- **Ask first:** "Which part feels the most confusing?"
- **Answer idea:** Break it into a few clear steps and tell them what happens at each one, and what they need to do (and don't). [PROOF]
- **Text:** Want me to send the steps from start to keys in plain English?
- **Don't say:** Jargon. 'It's easy.'

## 7. "We don't want to be stretched too thin or surprised by repairs."
- **Category:** Money & readiness
- **Behind it:** Fear of being house-poor or facing a big repair right after moving in.
- **Ask first:** "What would feel like a comfortable cushion after you move in?"
- **Answer idea:** Plan a payment that leaves room to breathe, use the inspection to spot big-ticket items, and set aside a repair cushion. Their lender helps with the numbers. [PROOF]
- **Text:** Let's plan a payment that still leaves you room to breathe. Want to map it out together?
- **Don't say:** Promises that a home won't need repairs. Budget advice beyond your role.
