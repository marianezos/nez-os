# Starter objections: Commercial (sales and leases)

NEZ OS starters for Stage 5. Rewrite each answer in the agent's voice, swap [PROOF] for one of their proof points (or [ADD PROOF]), and add the pushback they hear most. Ask whether they do commercial sales, leases or both, and build only the parts that fit. In the Objection Bank, use Client Type "Commercial" and start the Notes with "Sale" or "Lease."

**Note:** commercial deals often have different license, disclosure and contract rules. Only use these if the agent does commercial work. Lease terms, zoning, taxes, financials and legal questions go to their attorney, CPA, lender or the city. Still never describe or target people by a protected class.

## Commercial sales

## 1. "The price doesn't match the income."
- **Category:** Price
- **Behind it:** The property's income doesn't support the asking price on their numbers.
- **Ask first:** "What return do you need for this to make sense to you?"
- **Answer idea:** Respect their numbers. Work from the documents the seller provides (rent roll, expenses) and their own targets, and show what price makes it work. Their CPA and lender check the numbers. [PROOF]
- **Text:** Let's run it with your targets. Can you send me the return you need?
- **Don't say:** Projected returns as fact. Your own guesses about the property's finances.

## 2. "We need to see the financials before we do anything."
- **Category:** Trust & process
- **Behind it:** They want proof before spending time.
- **Ask first:** "Which documents matter most to you?"
- **Answer idea:** Agree completely. Explain how to request the documents, what's usually shared before and after an offer, and how to build a review period into the contract with their attorney. [PROOF]
- **Text:** Smart. Let me request the documents. Which ones matter most to you?
- **Don't say:** Pressure to offer before they've seen the numbers.

## 3. "We'll wait for interest rates to improve."
- **Category:** Market
- **Behind it:** Financing costs make the deal feel tight.
- **Ask first:** "At what rate or payment would this work for you?"
- **Answer idea:** Nobody can predict rates. Run the deal at today's financing with their lender, see what price or terms would make it work, and keep watching for a better fit. [PROOF]
- **Text:** Let's see what makes it work at today's rates. Want me to set up a call with a lender?
- **Don't say:** Rate predictions.

## 4. "Financing seems difficult."
- **Category:** Money & readiness
- **Behind it:** Commercial loans have different rules than home loans.
- **Ask first:** "Have you talked with a commercial lender yet?"
- **Answer idea:** Connect them with a commercial lender early so they know down payment, terms and what the lender needs, then shape the offer around it. [PROOF]
- **Text:** Commercial loans work differently. Want an intro to a lender who does them?
- **Don't say:** Loan or rate promises.

## 5. "We'll deal directly with the seller or their broker."
- **Category:** Other agent
- **Behind it:** They want to save money or keep it simple.
- **Ask first:** "Who's looking out for your side of the terms if you go direct?"
- **Answer idea:** Explain what you do for them: comparing options, reviewing the numbers, negotiating terms and keeping the timeline on track. [PROOF]
- **Text:** You can go direct. If you'd like someone on your side of the terms, I'm happy to help.
- **Don't say:** Anything negative about the seller or their broker.

## Commercial leases

## 6. "The rent is too high."
- **Category:** Price
- **Behind it:** They're comparing the headline rent, not the total cost.
- **Ask first:** "What total monthly cost works for your business, including everything?"
- **Answer idea:** Compare spaces on the same basis: base rent, extra charges, build-out, term length and incentives. Then negotiate where there's room. [PROOF]
- **Text:** Let's compare total cost, not just the rent. Want me to line up your options side by side?
- **Don't say:** Legal readings of lease terms.

## 7. "We'll just renew our current lease without looking around."
- **Category:** Timing
- **Behind it:** Moving feels like a hassle, and renewing feels safe.
- **Ask first:** "If you could change one thing about your current space or terms, what would it be?"
- **Answer idea:** Renewing may be the right call. Knowing the market first gives them leverage either way: compare a few options and use them in the renewal talk. [PROOF]
- **Text:** Renewing may be right. Want to see a few options first so you have leverage?
- **Don't say:** Pressure to move.

## 8. "We'll wait until the lease is almost up."
- **Category:** Timing
- **Behind it:** It feels too early to think about it.
- **Ask first:** "When does your lease end, and what notice does it require?"
- **Answer idea:** Explain why starting early helps: time to compare, negotiate and build out a new space, and to meet any notice dates in their lease (their attorney confirms those). [PROOF]
- **Text:** Starting early gives you options. Want to put a quick timeline together?
- **Don't say:** Legal advice on notice dates.

## 9. "A long lease is too much commitment."
- **Category:** Timing
- **Behind it:** Uncertainty about where the business is headed.
- **Ask first:** "Where do you see the business in two to three years?"
- **Answer idea:** Explain the trade-offs between short and long terms, and options they can ask their attorney about, like renewal, expansion or early-exit terms. [PROOF]
- **Text:** Let's map out where the business is headed, then find terms that fit. Free this week?
- **Don't say:** Legal advice.

## 10. "We'll just work with the landlord's broker."
- **Category:** Other agent
- **Behind it:** It seems simpler.
- **Ask first:** "Who's negotiating your side of the terms?"
- **Answer idea:** The landlord's broker works for the landlord. Explain what you do for a tenant: finding options, comparing total costs and negotiating terms. [PROOF]
- **Text:** You can. If you'd like someone on your side, I'm happy to help.
- **Don't say:** Anything negative about the landlord or their broker.

## 11. "Let's wait until business picks up."
- **Category:** Timing
- **Behind it:** Cash flow worries.
- **Ask first:** "What would need to happen for you to feel ready?"
- **Answer idea:** Respect it. Offer to keep watching spaces that fit their criteria and check back on a date they choose. [PROOF]
- **Text:** No rush. Want me to keep an eye out and check back on a date that works for you?
- **Don't say:** Predictions about the market or their business.
